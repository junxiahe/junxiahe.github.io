Hii, welcome to my website :)

I'm Junxia, a PhD Candidate in Operations Management at the University of Luxembourg under the supervision of Prof. Dr. Benny Mantin. I earned a BSc in Accounting (with a minor in CFA) from Nanjing Audit University and an MSc in Management Science and Engineering from Nanjing University of Finance and Economics. Before starting my doctorate, I gained research experience as a Research Assistant at the Nanjing University of Information Science & Technology.

My research interest sits at the intersection of sustainable operations management, retail operations and marketing, and game theory. My PhD project examines the Right to Repair regulation and its impacts from an operations management perspective. Working Papers

  • J. He, B. Mantin (2025). The Right to Repair: On the Impact of Mandated Reparability Level.
    AbstractHide abstract

    The Right to Repair (RTR) movement advocates for legislation that requires manufacturers to provide essential guides, tools and components that empower consumers to perform self-repairs. To that end, governments may mandate manufacturers to comply with a certain reparability level while making components available to avoid waste resulting from premature disposal of failed products. Does mandating reparability promote repairs and help reduce waste? As such legislation may trigger price responses by manufacturers, the answer is not clear. In this paper, we consider a two-period game-theoretic model where a manufacturer determines the price of a product as well as the price for repairs performed by the manufacturer (M-Repairs). Once a reparability level is mandated, consumers can also self-repair failed products (S-Repairs). We find that the manufacturer offers M-Repairs only when his repair cost is below a certain threshold and that mandating reparability can alleviate this threshold. Further, inducing S-Repairs may require mandating a high reparability level, which ultimately may also result in lower prices for the product as well as for the M-Repairs. While mandating reparability generally improves consumer surplus and reduces waste especially if the reparability level is high, it can potentially lead to an outcome where both the manufacturer's profit and consumer surplus shrink. Extending the model to include product durability as a decision variable, our numerical study reveals potential increases in waste (when reparability level is low) and profit, highlighting the necessity of a complementary durability regulation alongside mandated reparability levels.

  • J. He, B. Mantin, J. Veldman (2026). A Right to Spare Part: Pricing and Regulation.
    AbstractHide abstract

    How should manufacturers price their products and spare parts when consumers are forward-looking? We develop a game-theoretic model where demand for spare parts stems from the failure (modeled as a Binomial distribution) of purchased products. We show that the manufacturer may leverage the demand for spare parts as an instrument to price differentiate among consumers, whereby only consumers with high valuations are able to repair their failed products. This gives rise to potential social welfare-improving regulatory intervention such that constraints on pricing of repair parts are mandated.

  • J. He, L. Liang, B. Mantin (2026). Channel Competition under Mandated Reparability.
    AbstractHide abstract

    Manufacturers are facing increasing pressure to make repair services more convenient and affordable for the products they sell. Product reparability, promoted through regulation, has emerged as an instrument for independent repairers (IR) to perform repairs at a lower cost and higher recovered value. The mandated reparability intensifies competition between the repair channels operated by both manufacturers and IR. This gives rise to a strategic response from manufacturers: when the mandated reparability is sufficiently high, the manufacturer might decrease the product price during the post product failure period, which in turn encourages consumers to purchase a new product instead of repairing it. Besides, manufacturers can also provide an authorized repairer service. Given a certain reparability level, should manufacturers remain as competitors to compete in the repair market with independent repairers, or provide authorized repair service through a collaborating mode? he manufacturer responds by adjusting its new-product price and its repair price. We study this response in a model with a monopolist manufacturer, a strategic IR, and consumers whose preferences for the two repair channels are uniformly distributed along a Hotelling line. We solve consumers’ optimal purchasing decisions as an infinite-horizon Markov decision process. We develop a game-theoretic model in which repairs can be performed by either the manufacturer or an (authorized) independent repairer (IR). We investigate the manufacturer's pricing decisions across three scenarios characterized by different repair channel structures.
